Clarity

write like an aircraft manual

Aircraft mechanics write with a standard called ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English. They have used it since the 1980s. It makes an instruction mean one thing only. You can borrow the same rules to make AI output clear and easy to act on.

The content barely changes. The form changes. Hedge words go. One action goes in one sentence. A warning comes before the step.

The 6 rules

  1. Write one instruction per sentence.
  2. Keep procedural sentences to 20 words or fewer.
  3. Use the active voice. Use the imperative for steps.
  4. Use one term for one thing. Do not use synonyms.
  5. Do not hedge. If you are not sure, say so in one separate sentence.
  6. Put warnings and cautions before the step they apply to.

Where to use it

Use it for

  • Step-by-step guides and how-tos
  • Checklists and procedures
  • Output another tool must read
  • Readers who use English as a second language

Skip it for

  • Marketing and sales copy
  • Analysis that weighs trade-offs
  • Creative writing
  • Casual conversation

The bigger win

Most people use these rules to clean up what the AI writes. The larger gain is in what the AI reads. If an assistant answers from your notes or docs, write those in the same style. One name per thing and one claim per sentence help the assistant find the right passage.

Two limits

The model copies the standard. It does not check every word against the official dictionary. Short sentences do not make a wrong answer right. They only make a wrong answer easy to spot.
