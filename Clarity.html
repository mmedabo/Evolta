The content barely changes. The form changes. Hedge words go. One action goes on one line. A warning comes before the step.

The 6 rules
  • Write one instruction per sentence.
  • Keep procedural sentences to 20 words or fewer.
  • Use the active voice. Use the imperative for steps.
  • Use one term for one thing. Do not use synonyms.
  • Do not hedge. If you are not sure, say so in one separate sentence.
  • Put warnings and cautions before the step they apply to.

Where to use it

        

          

            
Use it for

            
  • Step-by-step guides
  • Checklists and procedures
  • Output a tool must read
  • Readers using English as a second language

          

          

            
Skip it for

            
  • Marketing and sales copy
  • Analysis that weighs trade-offs
  • Creative writing
  • Casual conversation

          

        

      


      

        
Good to know

        

          The bigger win
          Most people use these rules to clean up what the AI writes. The larger gain is in what the AI reads.
          If an assistant answers from your notes or docs, write those the same way. One name per thing and one claim per sentence help it find the right passage.
        

        

          Two limits
          The model copies the standard. It does not check every word against the official dictionary.
          Short sentences do not make a wrong answer right. They only make a wrong answer easy to spot.
