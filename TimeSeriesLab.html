AR, MA, ARMA & ARIMA

Cross-section data has no yesterday. Time series does — and that memory is the whole game. This lab builds the Box–Jenkins family one letter at a time: AR (memory of past values), MA (memory of past shocks), ARMA (both), and the I that makes a drifting series sit still long enough to be modelled. You'll tune coefficients with sliders, fire shocks and watch them echo, read ACF/PACF plots like a diagnostician, and forecast at the end.

The one-sentence version of each letter

Every model here is a recipe that turns an invisible stream of random shocks εt (mean 0, constant variance, no autocorrelation — "white noise") into the series you actually observe. The letters only differ in how long the recipe remembers.

AR(p)Autoregressive

Today is a weighted echo of its own past levels. One shock keeps ringing forever, decaying like φh.

MA(q)Moving average

Today is a weighted sum of the last few shocks. Memory is finite: after q periods the shock is gone, exactly.

ARMA(p,q)Both memories

A short-lived shock kick (MA) on top of a long decaying tail (AR). Most real series need a bit of each.

I(d)Integrated

The series wanders with no fixed mean. Difference it d times (Δyt = yt − yt−1) and model the result.

The notation, once
AR(p) yt = c + φ₁yt−1 + … + φpyt−p + εt Easier to read in deviation form: (yt − μ) = φ₁(yt−1 − μ) + … + εt, with μ = c / (1 − φ₁ − … − φp).
MA(q) yt = μ + εt + θ₁εt−1 + … + θqεt−q This lab uses the plus convention. Many textbooks and some software write −θ instead; the model is the same, the sign of θ̂ flips. Always check your package's convention.
ARMA(p,q) yt = c + Σφiyt−i + εt + Σθjεt−j Requires a stationary y. A small ARMA often beats a big pure AR — fewer parameters, same fit.
ARIMA(p,d,q) ARMA(p,q) fitted to Δdyt d = how many differences make it stationary. ARIMA(0,1,0) is the random walk; ARIMA(0,1,1) is exponential smoothing in disguise.
Stationarity: the licence to model

These models assume the mean, variance and autocorrelations don't depend on when you look — otherwise there is no stable thing to estimate, and the usual t-stats and R² mislead you (the classic spurious-regression trap). For an AR(1), stationarity means |φ| < 1: shocks die out. At φ = 1 you have a unit root — a random walk that never returns to a mean, whose variance grows with t. The fix is the I: difference it, then model the differences. Over-differencing has its own tell, and you'll meet it in Level 5.

Where this goes next

Natural follow-ups

  • Formal unit-root tests — ADF, KPSS: the statistical version of the eyeball test in Level 5.
  • Residual diagnostics — Ljung–Box Q on residual autocorrelation; a good model leaves white noise behind.
  • Order selection — AIC / BIC when ACF and PACF both just decay (the mixed-ARMA case from Level 6).
  • SARIMA — the same letters with a seasonal block, (P,D,Q)s, for monthly and quarterly data.
  • Beyond the mean — ARCH/GARCH when the variance is what clusters; VAR and cointegration for several series at once.
